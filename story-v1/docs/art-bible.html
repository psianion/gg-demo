0 · The drawing grammar

The film is one ink drawing that happens to live in three.js. Every asset below is built from five pencil words: sketch (solid + drawn edges), plate/outline (authored bezier silhouette), hatch (tone), score (interior line), ink (pressed-black fill), in three stroke weights. The renderer adds deterministic wobble and corner overshoot to every stroke; the drawings on this page run through the same kind of hand (ink displacement, restated lines, hatched shadow), so what you approve here is the feel the canvas ships.

  1. The 3D rule. This is a three.js film: the camera orbits, characters turn. Solid matter is never a camera-facing 2D cutout. Every piece declares a build class (table below) that guarantees its silhouette from any angle. Flat shapes are allowed only as symbolic light (ray bursts) or as thin real objects (an ear, a sign board: things that are genuinely plate-shaped and get real thickness).
  2. Two views or it doesn't exist. A design is only approved when its front and side read as the same character. The side profile of a revolved piece IS its lathe profile; what you approve here is literally what the code revolves.
  3. Weights. hero 2.4px = silhouettes the shot is about · form 1.5px = interior form, mid-ground · faint 0.85px = hatching, background, detail.
  4. Tone is hatching, never gradient. Values live in the fill washes below; shadows are drawn as parallel strokes.
  5. Ink (solid black) is rare: pupils, an open grin, the inside of a hearth. It is the loudest mark on the page.
  6. Monochrome first. Shapes get approved in greyscale. Colour is a later regrade of the same tokens (colour script already drafted in docs/color-script.md).

1 · Cast

The Goblin (hero)

Small, quick, kind. A real goblin drawn friendly, straight off the Pinterest character sheets: head + hood nearly half of him, long up-swept bat ears, big glinting eyes, small pointed nose, thin wide smile with one fang, wiry stick limbs with oversized hands and bare three-toed feet, scavenged asymmetric kit (one shoulder wrap). Hood stays on everywhere. The hood + ears silhouette must be readable at 20px tall (montage) and charming at full-screen (close-up).

Build
R skull + tunic are revolved from the approved side profile · X ears, hood tip, feet, mitts are thick authored silhouettes · S hood is a revolved cowl with a cut face-opening, rim drawn as a scored ring so the face stays framed from ±60°.
Proportion
≈2¼ heads tall · head + hood ≈ ½ of height · ear span > 2× body width, swept up-and-out · limbs wiry sticks; hands and feet oversized · a slight ready-bend at the knees even at rest.
Rig
Joint hierarchy kept (pelvis/bob/chest/neck/head, pose+swing); RIG bone lengths retune to this sheet at build (shorter legs, bigger head offset, longer ears) — the reach solvers read RIG, so they follow. Fore-aft gait restored (rotation.x).
Face
Eyes = big whites + ink pupils with a paper glint (blink via scale) · one soft brow-ridge stroke per side, never a scowl · nose = small pointed wedge · smile = thin wide curve, one fang up at the corner · all on the existing expression channels.
Tokens
tunic wash-5 · skin wash-4 · hood/satchel b8 · wraps c6 · claws wash-6.

KEEP: verified close-up under the lit render — full card checklist reads.

Cliff remains · cairn

The cliff-hook opening's dressing, no sheet for it before now: storybook aftermath, no gore. A skirmish happened at this rim, and the same spots later read "tended" — that's the bookend the film sells. Battle-remains state: arrow shafts stuck in the rim and the near face, crosshatch scorch on both, a small round shield dropped where it fell, a trampled patch. Tended state: a small cairn of five faceted flat stacked stones (never smooth blobs, that read as a soft-serve swirl and was rejected) plus a three-mushroom cluster, never symmetric.

Read
Both states have to read from the opening's low front camera looking up at the rim AND from the C6 over-shoulder looking down at it — the rim dressing sits on the near lip for exactly that reason, since a figure standing on the far edge of a flat plateau is occluded by its own top face from below.
Build
S arrow shafts + fletch, dropped shield disc + boss, all small assemblies · X crosshatch patches for scorch and trample, flat to the surface they mark · S cairn stones are low-sided roughened drums, the cliff's own tiering technique, not lathed blobs — faceted and flat-topped so the stack reads as slabs, not a swirl.
Tokens
cairn stones wash-4 (stoneCool) · shield wash-3 body (steel), wash-4 boss (iron) · arrow shaft wash-5 (timberDark), fletch paper (snow) · scorch and trample stay plain ink hatch, no fill.

KEEP: card drafted from the shipped build; both states verified in-shot.

4 · Sign-off process

  1. Redline anything on this page, shapes, proportions, verdicts. I redraw here, in the bible, not on canvas.
  2. When a card is approved it gets built exactly to its sheet (profiles traced from these drawings) in its declared build class, one card at a time, goblin first.
  3. Canvas verification per card: front, side, and turn (three angles in Chrome) before moving on.
  4. Colour pass happens only after every card is shape-approved, as a palette regrade.

# THE UNDERDRAWING — 3D creative direction for the Good Goblin story site

2026-09-02 · v2 direction. Keeps the story, the beats, the two-state curtain grammar, the
goblin, and the no-cut ending. Reimagines everything about how it looks, moves, and converts.
Supersedes `art-direction.md`'s execution notes (its grammar stands); `story.md` and
`copy-spec.md` remain law. Build requires an approved plan; this document is the direction.

---

## 0. Where we are, honestly

The film's structure is a 9/10: cold open, "That was me.", three proofs (light, scenery,
time), montage, flatten, board, no cut. The curtain grammar reads on screen today — the
lantern's amber pool re-igniting grey trees is already the best frame on the site.

What's on screen around it is previz: infinite bare ground planes, faceted lathe primitives
with visible construction, one flat fill per surface, a decal disc for "light", no shadows,
no atmosphere, captions floating over scenery. Three sculpt passes proved the ceiling:
procedural forms and flat fills will never read as *made*. The story says a craftsman
prepared this world; the pixels must look like one did.

---

## 1. Core concept — THE UNDERDRAWING

**Every world you've ever played in was drawn by someone first.**

The site is a painted storybook world that keeps freezing into its own pencil underdrawing.
In motion, you watch the Performance: paint, wind, firelight, a living world. When time
stops, you see the Prep: the same world as its maker's working drawing — the identical
brushstrokes, unpainted; construction lines still in place; and beneath the ground, faint
as a watermark, **a battle grid**. The world has been sitting on a battlemap the whole time.

Only the goblin is painted on both sides of the curtain. He is the one who crosses.

At the end the metaphor stops being a metaphor. The world flattens into the drawing, the
drawing is a Good Goblin map, and the pencil is handed to you. "The map you drew is the
game you run" — the film is that sentence, told backwards, in one take.

**Two named states** (rendering language, product language, same words):

| | THE PERFORMANCE | THE PREP |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The world as players experience it | The world as its maker sees it |
| Surface | Painted gouache over paper tooth | Graphite underdrawing on the same paper |
| Light | Lit, warm keys, real shadows | Flat paper light, **no shadows** |
| Motion | Everything breathes on the world clock | The clock is stopped |
| The grid | Invisible | Ghosted under everything |
| The goblin | In it | Alive in it, in full color, casting the only shadow |

Restraint rule (unchanged from the original grammar): the contrast IS the storytelling.
The Prep appears only when we're allowed backstage. If it were everywhere it would mean
nothing.

## 2. Narrative — the site's arc

The script and all twelve captions stay exactly as `copy-spec.md` locks them. What the
direction adds is a *shape*: the film is a claim, three proofs, and a handover.

1. **The claim** (cold open → "Straight to work."): every campaign's shared memory — the
   goblin that got away — inverted. First freeze. First sight of the underdrawing and the
   grid ghost. The audience is told: you're backstage now.
2. **Proof of light** (lantern run): someone has to light the world. The re-ignition arc —
   color returns to whatever he touches.
3. **Proof of scenery** (theatre turn): the forest rotates away like a set because it *is*
   a set. In the freeze, the under-stage machinery — capstan, rails, counterweights — is
   visible in graphite. Reality has rigging.
4. **Proof of time** (the die, the centerpiece): natural 1, *tick*, silence, the nudge,
   natural 20, "You're welcome." Played exactly as scripted. This is the beat people will
   send each other; it gets the deepest polish budget.
5. **The many jobs** (montage, silent): six island vignettes, quick cuts, no captions.
6. **The handover** (cliff → flatten → board → endcard): the world lies down into its own
   underdrawing, the underdrawing fills with the map's paint, the d20 lands 20, and the
   board under the wordmark is a *real, authored Good Goblin map* — the film's last frame
   is the product's first frame. No cut. CTA.

## 3. Visual world — islands on the page

The single biggest staging change: **the world is built on islands.**

Every chapter set is a hand-built diorama — a finite piece of world with a sculpted edge:
cut earth strata, roots trailing off, a path that ends in a pencil line that simply stops
being drawn. The islands float on the page; the paper is the void. No infinite ground
planes anywhere, ever again.

Why this is right and not just pretty:

- It's the *truth of the story* — the goblin builds sets; the theatre chapter already
  rotates a forest on a disc; the montage already lives on platforms. We're promoting the
  film's best existing instinct to the whole world.
- It kills the previz read structurally. An empty plane looks unfinished; a bounded
  diorama with a designed edge looks *intentional* at any density.
- It rhymes with the tabletop. These are the best painted miniatures you've seen, at
  world scale — and when the flatten comes, a diorama collapsing into its map icon feels
  inevitable instead of clever.

World inventory stays the story's: night ambush camp under a cliff; the morning path;
the theatre disc and its snowy mountain backstage; the tavern interior; six montage
vignettes; the dawn cliff over a miniature world; the board. Dressing density triples:
grass tufts, stones, stumps, roots, ferns, cart ruts, moths, hung laundry — the specific
clutter of a world someone maintains. The goblin design is locked (v2: one-piece olive
cloak, wing ears, big amber eyes behind round rims). The adventurer trio stays soft
supporting cast — silhouette-first, never upstaging.

The page itself is part of the world: a quiet margin around the viewport with tick marks
and a penciled chapter numeral (C1…C7) that lives at whisper opacity during the
Performance and breathes fully legible during every freeze — the storyboard sheet the
world is drawn on.

## 4. Typography — the hand, the inscription, the quiet serif

Type obeys the curtain like everything else. Three voices, strict roles:

1. **The Hand — authored lettering, not a font.** All twelve narration captions plus the
   endcard tagline are hand-lettered once, as stroke SVGs in the same hand that drew the
   wordmark — a confident storyteller's ink hand, mixed case, slightly narrow, feet
   planted. Captions *write on* stroke-by-stroke (dash reveal, ~0.4s, power2.out) and
   un-write on reverse scroll. In Prep state they render graphite; in Performance beats
   (the two pitch lines) they render ink. Twelve lines is a weekend of lettering and it
   buys what no webfont can: narration that is unmistakably *drawn by the same maker as
   the world*. This replaces Metamorphous everywhere.
2. **The Inscription — Cinzel 700 caps, letterspaced.** Reserved for exactly one job:
   the carved voice of the CTA button and the tiny wayfinding labels (play / scroll /
   replay, set as small caps). Matches the engraved d20 direction in `docs/logo/`.
3. **The Quiet Serif — Vollkorn (variable, true italic), self-hosted.** The endcard
   subline, the skip note, fallbacks, and anything longer than a sentence. Warm, bookish,
   invisible — a picture-book colophon voice. Its italic exists, so we finally get one
   without synthesis. (Amended 2026-09-02 by the hero doc: the original pick, Newsreader,
   sits on the impeccable reflex-reject list.)

Hierarchy is carried by voice, not size. Nothing on the page is ever set in a grotesk.
Caption discipline from `copy-spec.md` holds: lower-third band, phone-verified clamps,
≥4.5:1 on paper.

## 5. Color palette — paper, graphite, and one alive color

The color script (`color-script.md`) remains the per-chapter law for the painted world.
The site-level system above it:

**The spine (both states)**
- `PAPER` warm bone `#F2EBDD` — the page, the void, the sky's floor
- `TOOTH` `#E4DAC8` — paper grain shadow, margin furniture
- `GRAPHITE` `#5A544A` — underdrawing strokes, Prep midtone
- `INK` warm near-black `#26221C` — contours, lettering, the wordmark

**The world (Performance, per chapter script)** — deep forest greens, ochre earth, indigo
night, cool morning blue-green, overcast stage grey, tavern lamplight browns, dawn gold.
Storybook watercolor: mixed, muted, never neon.

**The alive ramp** — the film's emotional accent is **lantern amber**: `#F2C35E` (the
goblin's eyes) through `#E8963E` (lantern glass) to `#C75B2A` (ember). Amber = life,
touch, fire, the nudged die, the lit CTA. It is the only color allowed to glow.

**The goblin** — sage skin `#84A058`, olive cloak ramp, amber eyes. Green belongs to him
alone; nothing else in the world may read goblin-green, so he is findable in every frame.

**Brand green `#B6D648`** — marketing-only hue: the wordmark's d20 pip and nothing else.
It never tints world or surfaces.

Contrast floors: captions ≥4.5:1 against their band; the grid ghost stays ≤4% ink over
paper so it reads as watermark, not floor tile.

## 6. Composition — storyboard discipline

- **The four-plane rule.** Every shot has a foreground occluder (grass blades, a branch,
  a tent rope — in Prep these render as near-black graphite silhouettes), a midground
  subject, a supporting element, and a backdrop flat. No frame is ever floor-and-sky.
- **One eye-path per shot.** A single subject, a single secondary. The lantern shot is
  *about* the lantern; the chest and door wait their turn. (This fixes the current C2
  scatter.)
- **The goblin sits on thirds**, low half of frame at his scale moments, dead-center only
  for direct address ("That was me.", the shrug, "You're welcome.").
- **Captions live in the lower-third band on the page margin system** — never over a set
  piece. If a caption would cross scenery, the framing moves, not the caption.
- **The page margin replaces letterboxing.** Full-bleed 3D inside a breathing sheet
  (≈24–40px), tick marks, penciled chapter numeral. Portrait keeps the 70° FOV clamp +
  pull-back; islands restack vertically for tall aspect instead of cropping.

## 7. Materials — the painted trilayer

Per the standing directive: real painted texture, no procedural bakes, no hatching. Every
world surface carries three authored maps over simple sculpted forms:

1. **Paint** — gouache albedo with visible, directional brushwork that follows form
   (bark strokes run the trunk; roof strokes run the fall line). Paper tooth shows
   through at grazing angles: everything is painted *on the page*, thinly.
2. **Underdrawing** — the same forms' graphite layer: contour passes, construction
   ellipses, measure ticks, a stray erased line. Revealed only by the curtain. One asset,
   two truths — the freeze literally lifts the paint off the drawing.
3. **Wear** — edge-lightening on silhouette edges and handled corners (a miniature
   painter's drybrush): the world is *maintained*, and the wear says whose hands do it.

Shading is lit Lambert. Thin single-weight ink contours stay. Surface hatching stays
banned everywhere, at every value, forever. Carpentry keeps straight edges; organics keep
sculpted lumps; the die is a jewel — painted numerals, worn edges, a bright authored
glint, because it is the story's other protagonist.

Texture sourcing: hand-painted or approved-generation only, quality-gated on the art
bible; **no forge runs without in-the-moment approval**. The bible page remains the
sign-off surface, extended with material boards (bark / roof / ground / cloth swatch
sheets) before any set is painted.

## 8. Lighting — two rigs, one rule

**Performance rig** — per-chapter scripts, finally honored: C1 is genuinely *night* — one
fire, warm pool, indigo dark, embers; C2 cool morning warming as the lantern run
progresses; C3 overcast, stage-lit-by-nothing; C4 the film's only interior — lamplight
pools, dark corners; C5 bright, flat, weightless; C6 dawn gold at altitude with painted
god-ray cones; C7 paper light. One soft shadow-casting key per island, contact blobs
under props, firelight as flickering warm points (the flicker rig exists) plus painted
glow cones — light is allowed to be a *painted object*, never a lens flare.

**Prep rig** — flat, even paper light and **no shadows: a drawing casts none.** The
single exception carries the whole grammar: the goblin, and anything he has touched,
keeps a private warm key and a soft contact shadow. In any frozen frame, the only shadow
on the page belongs to what is alive. When he touches the lantern, its shadow arrives
with its color.

## 9. 3D elements — the cast of things

Hero tier (deepest polish): the **goblin** (locked v2 design; cloak hem physics, ear
acting, eye glints), the **d20** (jewel treatment, real inertia, the wobble, the nudge),
the **lantern kit** (lamppost — always a lantern post, never a torch — hand lantern,
campfire, tavern lamps).

Set tier: seven chapter islands with sculpted edges and tripled dressing; the theatre
machinery (rope, capstan, under-stage gears — graphite-only, freeze-visible); the snowy
mountain backstage flat; the miniature world under the C6 cliff (tilt-shift moment); the
final board as a real authored map.

Grammar tier (new): the **underdrawing layer** on every asset; the **grid ghost** under
every island; **margin furniture** (ticks, chapter numerals, the skip note); particle
language — dust motes in lamplight, moths at lanterns, embers over fires, drifting
cloud flats — Performance-only, all frozen mid-air by the tick.

Supporting cast: adventurer trio, ravens, tavern patrons — silhouette-first, low detail
by design, never colored more richly than the goblin.

## 10. Depth — two languages, one per state

**Performance depth is atmospheric:** distance desaturates and cools toward paper (fog
ramps to `PAPER`, never grey), parallax cloud/hill flats at the back (honest theatre
flats — allowed for atmosphere, banned for solid matter), and shallow tilt-shift DOF at
exactly two moments: the die close-up and the C6 miniature-world reveal, where the
scale-flip is the story.

**Prep depth is graphite:** no fog, no DOF. Distance is line-weight falloff — near
contours dark and full, far strokes fainter with more paper show-through — aerial
perspective as a pencil renders it. Foreground occluders go near-black silhouette. This
is a depth cue the web hasn't seen, and it costs a uniform, not a pass.

The camera vocabulary stays cinematic and authored: rails, push-ins on freezes, the
angle-echo bookend at the cliff, the vertical rise into the flatten.

## 11. Motion language — two clocks and a tick

- **The world clock** runs every ambient thing — fire, leaves, moths, cloth, clouds. It
  is one clock, so the freeze is one event: same-frame, everything, silence. No slow-mo
  easing into freezes; the *tick* is instant. (Architecture exists; it stays the heart.)
- **The goblin clock never stops.** He is alive by construction, not by exception.
- **Scroll is the reader's finger.** Nothing plays itself; scrolling backwards un-tells
  the tale, un-writes the lettering, un-lights the lanterns. The play button remains as
  assisted scroll for people who want to lean back. Lenis scrub ≈0.8 stays.
- **House ease:** power2.out — confident deceleration — for every settle; the only
  overshoots in the film belong to the goblin's own acting (the dust-off, the shrug).
- **The boil:** hand-drawn UI strokes (margin ticks, caption lettering at rest, the CTA's
  offset border) re-draw themselves at 2 frames / ~6fps at whisper amplitude — the
  storybook shimmer that says *drawn*, never *rendered*. Off under reduced motion.
- **Micro-interactions get hero-prop polish** (the standing "every micro-interaction
  perfect" bar): the CTA's touch-lighting, the endcard wordmark's one-time ear-perk on
  hover, the die settling to 20 when the scroll hint idles. Nothing else. Three perfect
  beats over thirty clever ones.
- **Reduced motion:** freezes become cuts, write-ons become fades, the boil stops, the
  film remains fully legible as a stepped picture-book.

Sound stays out of scope by design; the direction reserves exactly one future sound — the
tick — and nothing else.

## 12. Conversion goal — hand them the pencil

**One goal: closed-beta Discord joins.** One CTA, no waitlist, no backend, no feature
list. Everything above exists to make one specific person — a DM, or the player who
always wondered who lights the lanterns — feel *seen backstage*, then step into it.

The conversion moment is the grammar's final inversion:

- The board under the endcard is a **real authored Good Goblin map** in the product's
  own art style — product truth as the final material. What you land on is what you get.
- Endcard stays `copy-spec.md` direction C — lettered on the board, no box: wordmark,
  hand-lettered tagline ("The Dungeon Master's backstage."), quiet-serif subline, CTA.
- **The CTA is drawn, not lit: graphite outline, paper fill — until your cursor touches
  it.** On hover/press it takes color the way everything the goblin touches does — warm
  lantern amber blooming through the letterforms, contact shadow arriving with it. The
  visitor performs the film's grammar as their final act. On click, the die beside the
  wordmark rolls a natural 20 as the send-off, and Discord opens in a new tab.
- **Respect for return visitors:** after the claim lands (post–"Straight to work."), a
  penciled margin note appears bottom-right — *skip to the table ↓* — jumping to the
  endcard. Award juries scrub; DMs are busy; both are served.
- Measurement: none shipped until a decision is made (no analytics scripts by default;
  the site currently has no backend and that stays true for launch).

`DISCORD_URL` remains the single wired constant (owner: Nayan).

## 13. What this is not

- Not a tech demo: no chrome blobs, no particle typography, no cursor trails, no
  shader-flex hero, no dark-mode neon. The flex is *craft* — paper, paint, pencil,
  executed in real 3D with two rendering truths.
- Not a copy of the scroll-through-a-world genre: the ownable five are the underdrawing
  curtain, islands-on-the-page, the only-shadow-of-the-alive rule, hand-lettered
  narration obeying the curtain, and the touch-lit conversion into a real product map.
  Any beat that doesn't serve one of those five gets cut before it gets polished.
- Not photoreal, not pixel-art, not flat vector (product art bans hold here too).

## 14. Guardrails carried forward

- Surface hatching: banned, hard, everywhere (standing directive). Tone comes from light.
- Real painted textures only; **no generation runs without in-the-moment approval**.
- Goblin v2 design and all locked copy stay as approved; the art bible (extended with
  material boards) remains the sign-off surface before sets are painted.
- Nothing deploys, publishes, or leaves this machine; `story/` stays uncommitted; there
  is still no deployment decision and this document does not make one.
- Any build begins only after a plan for it is approved.

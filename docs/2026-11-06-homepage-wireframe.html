The island, the table, and the goblin who sets it

A line diagram of every pinned frame in Script v2, from hero to the waitlist, plus the ordinary page after it unpins. This is a layout and motion sketch: no art, no colour except the orange player tokens from your sketches and brass for notes and camera moves.

Carry over from story/The hero island: hero.ts, heroLife.ts, islandKit.ts. The stroke and paint style: pencil.ts, trilayer.ts, rigs.ts, palette.ts. The scroll plumbing (Lenis + GSAP) and the fonts.
DropThe 66 s timeline and gearing.ts, every S1–S8 set, the adventurer cast, the current goblin model (goblin.ts, rig.ts), and the old LOCKED docs.
Keep as ideas onlyThe dice gag (freeze, shrug, nudge to 20), the goblin's action vocabulary (rope, lantern, dust-off), and the "board appears with no cut" ending trick.

Beat 0 · unit 0

Hero: island in the air

Reuse

This is the shipped hero island. Its cursor and idle systems (heroLife.ts) already exist. The goblin inside it has to be swapped for the new design.

Add

The rope hanging off the edge, and the lantern post, which must exist here so it can be lit in Beat 2.

Idea: the cursor works as the lantern's light. Where you hover, the island warms slightly, which sets up the Light toggle before the visitor has seen it.
Beat 1 · units 0 → 2

Landing: he docks the island

The handoff from hover rotation to scroll control is the hard part. The island has to ease from wherever the mouse left it, or it will snap.

Idea: the grid could be drawn in by a pencil stroke sweeping across the page, using the existing pencil.ts line style, rather than a plain fade. It tells the visitor that this is a map.
Beat 2 · units 2 → 8

Canvas showcase

This is the longest beat and the most expensive to build. There are three toggles, and each changes real island state, with goblin animation covering the change.

Cheapest version that still works

Ship Light for real (it doubles as dark mode). Ship Weather and Encounters as scripted scroll sequences with replay buttons, then upgrade them later.

Idea: mark which icon has been "already used" with a small pencil tick. A visitor who scrolled past all three without clicking then gets a gentle nudge: two icons are still unticked.
Beat 3 · units 8 → 13

The table

Tokens arrive and the fog is revealed, then the dice gag. The gag is the emotional peak, so it should get the most polish.

Idea: let the visitor roll the die themselves (click it or press space). It always comes up 1 and the goblin always fixes it. That makes it the one interaction the visitor will remember and share, and it's cheap because the outcome is fixed.
Idea: the "pencil freeze" is a free effect, because the stroke style already has a pencil layer. Freezing becomes switching the paint layer off, with no new shader.
Beat 4 · units 13 → 16

Pull-back: it was on the DM's laptop

This is the hardest technical move. The 3D island must keep rendering inside a screen that shrinks into a room, which means render-to-texture onto the laptop plane at the hand-off point.

Keep it cheap

Make the room 2D ink cards (people, lamp, table) layered in depth. Only the laptop screen is live 3D.

Idea: over the DM's shoulder is the right framing. The visitor is now the DM, which is the product's pitch in one picture.
Beat 5 · units 16 → 18

The screen: where you join

This is the only conversion point. Put the email field first and keep it plain: a confirmation line, no modal.

Open

"Real product canvas" needs either a live embed or a pixel-matched still. Start with a still.

Idea: some visitors won't scroll 18 screens. Put a small "Join the waitlist" chip in the top-right corner for the whole pinned stage, so nobody has to watch the whole film to sign up.
After unit 18

Feature sections

Each feature gets three lines, one image and nothing else. The stills are frames rendered from the island scene, so they cost no new art.

Idea: clicking the mini island should rewind the whole film in about 1.5 seconds and land back at the hero, not jump there. It's a small reward for anyone who scrolled all the way down.

Questions for you before the art pass

  • Goblin design. The script needs two or three reference images from you. Every frame here uses a placeholder stick goblin with big ears.
  • Beat 2 scope. Do all three toggles work for real at launch, or is Light the only real one with the other two scripted? I recommend Light only.
  • Visitor rolls the die? Do you want the click-to-roll version of the dice gag?
  • Waitlist backend. Where do the emails go?
